# What I learned

From bottom half guard, the entry is an **underhook** — the grip may start around the belt or waist — combined with using the foot to hook or trap the opponent's leg, then coming up toward the **dogfight** position. From there we drilled three finishes.

# The technique

1. **Roll-through / Plan B variation** — thread the arm under and through the opponent's legs and roll underneath, using their pressure and keeping control of the trapped leg to come up on top.
2. **Far-leg roll-under variation** — from dogfight, reach behind and under the opponent's leg and roll inward to reverse them. Exact grip and direction still need confirming.
3. **Forward-driving dogfight sweep** — keep the leg trapped, come up, and drive forward into the opponent to topple them and finish on top.

## Names are tentative

- The position/entry is the **half-guard underhook to dogfight**.
- The first rolling finish sounds like the **Plan B sweep**, **roll-under sweep**, or **roll-through dogfight sweep**.
- The under-leg threading finish is also described as a **rollover/roll-through dogfight sweep** in some instructionals; names vary by instructor.
- The forward drive is commonly called a **dogfight sweep** or **underhook dogfight sweep**. If it involves controlling the far foot/ankle and circling over the trapped leg, it may be the **old-school sweep**.

These are best-effort matches from the description, not a definitive identification — confirm the exact grips and direction with the instructor.
